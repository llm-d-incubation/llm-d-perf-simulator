How a prediction is made

First

Price one forward pass

A calibrated cost model takes a model graph, a chip and fitted coefficients, and returns the time for a single pass.

Then

Sweep the configurations

Parallelism, instance counts and batch limits are swept across the accelerator budget, each candidate scored under your workload.

Finally

Keep what is not dominated

Candidates beaten on every metric are dropped; what remains is the frontier you pick a deployment from.

Where this fits

BLIS is an incubating component of llm-d, the Kubernetes-native stack for distributed LLM inference. llm-d deploys and scales serving engines across a cluster; BLIS answers the question that comes first, which is what to deploy. The engine and routing flags a search returns, things like max-num-seqs, max-num-batched-tokens and tensor-parallel width, are the knobs you then set on a real deployment.
